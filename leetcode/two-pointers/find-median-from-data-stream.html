Algorithm Logic & Theory

The Find Median from Data Stream problem asks us to design a data structure that calculates the median of a flowing sequence of numbers. Using a sorted array would take $O(N)$ to insert a number. We can optimize this dramatically using a Two Heaps (Priority Queue) pattern.

Core Concept:

  • Divide the Data into Halves
    We maintain two heaps: a Max-Heap (`smallList`) to store the smaller half of the numbers, and a Min-Heap (`largeList`) to store the larger half.
  • Maintain the Ordering Property
    Every time we add a number, we first throw it into the Max-Heap. But to ensure that every number in the Max-Heap is genuinely smaller than every number in the Min-Heap, we immediately poll the largest element from the Max-Heap and offer it to the Min-Heap.
  • Maintain the Size Balance
    We enforce a rule: the Max-Heap (`smallList`) is allowed to have at most 1 more element than the Min-Heap (`largeList`). If the Min-Heap ever gets bigger, we poll its smallest element and push it back to the Max-Heap.
  • Extracting the Answer in $O(1)$
    Because of our balancing act, the median is always readily available at the roots of our heaps. If the total number of elements is odd, the median is the root of the Max-Heap. If even, it's the average of the roots of both heaps.
Time Complexity addNum: O(log N)
findMedian: O(1)

Inserting a number into a heap takes $O(\log N)$ time. We do this at most three times per insertion. Finding the median just requires peeking at the roots, which takes $O(1)$ time.

Space Complexity O(N)

Where $N$ is the total number of elements added from the data stream. We are storing all the elements across the two Priority Queues.
